Features

Everything the factory firmware wouldn't do.

Built on QMK for this exact board. Every pin and the Bluetooth module's protocol were worked out from the original firmware, not guessed.

Lighting without flicker

Keys refresh about 1,400 times a second and the side lights about 3,100, past the IEEE 1789 flicker guidance. Choose from 20 effects in a gallery of live previews.

Settings over Bluetooth

Change settings with the cable unplugged, and see changes made with the Fn keys, the device in use and the battery in the app.

Macros on Fn + any key

Text, keys, shortcuts and pauses. Record them by typing and try them in the app. They're stored on the keyboard, and Ctrl ↔ Cmd swaps by itself on a Mac.

Windows or Mac, per device

Each of the 5 Bluetooth devices and the cable keeps its own layout, so a Mac and a PC can share one keyboard.

A battery you can see

See the level, charging and the cable on the Power page. Deep sleep when idle, and the key that wakes it is still typed.

Safe, reversible updates

One-click installs, checked against the release, with your settings and macros put back afterwards. The original firmware is always one click away.

VIA compatible

Configure it in VIA in the browser, with nothing to install. Load the provided JSON once to remap all 5 layers, edit macros and reach every Keycandle setting.

Reverse-engineered

The pins, LEDs and Bluetooth protocol came from disassembling the original firmware. Everything it did is kept, and then improved.

The app

One window for the whole keyboard.

A native app written in Rust with GPUI, the GPU-accelerated framework behind the Zed editor: no browser inside, instant to start. Live previews, instant changes, saved on the keyboard. In English, Spanish and Portuguese, in light or dark.

Overview: the keyboard as it is now, and everything that's set.

How it works

Two ways to talk to the keyboard.

Over the cable it's the standard VIA protocol. Over Bluetooth there's no data channel at all, so Keycandle makes one out of things every keyboard already has.

Over the USB cable

AppVIAKeyboard
  • Reads and writes every setting, macros and the keymap
  • Follows changes made with the Fn keys within half a second
  • Installs firmware: the chip's bootloader is USB-only

Over Bluetooth

Applock lightsKeyboard
Keyboardmedia codesApp
  • Settings are spelled out by flipping the Num, Caps and Scroll lights (the lock states don't change)
  • The keyboard answers with media-key codes that no system acts on
  • Checked and confirmed, about 1.3 s to reconnect, and typing is never blocked
The firmware

Factory vs. Keycandle.

With no schematic or source to go on, the original V1.12 firmware was disassembled to learn the pin map, the LED drivers, the Bluetooth module's protocol and every factory feature. Keycandle keeps all of it, on the same keyboard and the same Bluetooth pairings, and does a lot more.

Get started

Up and running in a minute.

For the RK71 RGB with Bluetooth (PCB 603-836/RGB-BT-HFD, chip SN32F248BF).

Download the app

For Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) or Linux. There's nothing to install on Windows.

Plug in the cable

The app finds the keyboard, even on its original firmware.

Click Install

On the Firmware page. The keyboard restarts on its own, and then you're set, over the cable or Bluetooth.

Is it safe? Can I go back?

Yes. The chip's bootloader is in ROM, so installing can't erase it. The app restores the original firmware with one click, and there's a step-by-step recovery guide for the worst case.

Do my Bluetooth pairings survive?

Yes. They live in the Bluetooth module, which no firmware install touches.

Why is there no battery percentage?

The Bluetooth module only reports high, medium or low, and the board has no wire to measure the battery. Keycandle shows exactly what the hardware knows.

Does it work with VIA?

Yes: load the VIA definition from the release once, and VIA in Chrome or Edge configures everything over the cable, including macros.

macOS says it can't check the app

It's open source but not notarized by Apple. Open System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. For Bluetooth settings, allow Input Monitoring when asked.
